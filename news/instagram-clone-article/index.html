Someone Cloned My Father's Instagram. Then They Did It Again.

Created on April 06, 2026

2026

My father is not a public figure. He’s a regular person with a modest Instagram following — mostly family, old colleagues, and friends from his community. So when the first fake account appeared, we thought it was a one-off. We reported it. We blocked it. Done.

Except it wasn’t done.

A new clone showed up within days. Same name with a double underscore. Different photos, taken from God knows where. And this time, some of his friends had already received a message:

“Hey, I’m in a difficult situation. Can you send me some money? I’ll explain later.”

By the time we caught the third account, we stopped treating this as a nuisance and started treating it as a serious problem. This post is everything we’ve learned — not just from our experience, but from researching how this actually works and what you can realistically do about it.

Why Cloners Target “Regular” People

You might think: Why bother cloning someone with only 300 followers?

  1. Lower Guard: People are less suspicious when a real friend reaches out than when a celebrity does.
  2. Personal Connection: A message from “Dad” or “Uncle” has instant emotional leverage.
  3. Automated Tools: Scammers use bots to scrape and clone thousands of accounts at once. It’s a volume game.

Our Step-by-Step Response Plan

1. Document Before You Report

Before you hit that report button, take screenshots of:

  • The fake profile (showing the handle and bio).
  • The posts (to show they are stolen).
  • Any direct messages sent to friends.

These are your receipts if you need to escalate to the platform’s support or local authorities.

2. Warn the Network Publicly and Immediately

We posted a warning on my father’s real account and sent a group message to his “Close Friends” list.

What to say: “A fake account using my name and photos is messaging people asking for money. Please do not respond or send any funds. My only account is this one.”

3. Report the Impersonation (The Right Way)

On Instagram, choose: Report > Something about this account > They are pretending to be someone else > Me / Someone I know.

If you have a business or creator account, your reporting might get faster traction.

4. Enable 2FA and Privacy Settings

While 2FA doesn’t stop cloning, it prevents the scammer from hacking your actual account to make the clone look even more convincing.

We switched my father’s account to Private. This prevents scammers from easily scraping new photos for future clones.

5. Report to Authorities (If Money Was Involved)

If someone actually sent money, this is no longer just a “terms of service” violation — it’s a crime.

  • In Canada: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (antifraudcentre.ca) or the National Cybercrime Online Reporting System (cyber.gc.ca)
  • In the US: IC3.gov
  • In India: cybercrime.gov.in

The Bottom Line

The more we talk openly about how these scams work, the harder they become to pull off. Share this with someone who needs it.
